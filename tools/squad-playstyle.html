Fights — wer eröffnet

A fight is “our squad against one enemy team” — hover a column title for what the number means. The last row, Enemy opens, is the yardstick: the same fights when the enemy shoots first.

How a fight is counted
One fight
Everything between us and one enemy team until 45 seconds pass without a hit. After that, the next exchange with the same team is a new fight.
Opened
Whoever lands the first hit opened the fight. If the enemy shoots first, the fight does not appear in this table at all.
Who gets credited
The row belongs to whoever opened. “Enemies per fight” is what the whole squad achieves afterwards, “by himself” only his own work. Opening a lot but downing little means he pulls the others in — whether that pays off shows in “own losses per fight”.
An enemy “down”
One player put on the floor, not one event: a knock and the later finishing blow are the same thing. If he gets revived and drops again, that counts anew.
Bots
Excluded by default — otherwise every rate looks better than it is. The switch above pulls them back in.
Enemy opens
All fights someone else started against us, across every match in range. Read the rows above against it — an opening rate only means something next to what happens when you get jumped.
Faded rows
Fewer than five opened fights: too little to conclude anything. Those rows get no highlight either.

Where these numbers come from
Distance to team
Every ten seconds the distance to the nearest mate is measured; the column shows the typical value.
Distance when downed
Taken from the 25 seconds before the knock. Measure around the knock instead and you measure the mate running in to revive — everything then looks harmless.
Standing still
Less than five metres between two samples counts as standing. Only the second half of a life is counted, because standing early is looting.
What is missing
The database keeps positions for our own squad only. There are no movement traces for enemies, so no comparison against them.
